## At a glance

| To                                      | Write                                                        |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| declare a many-to-many association      | `hasMany(Article, { through: "taggings" })`                  |
| follow an association with another name | `hasMany(Author, { through: "articles", source: "author" })` |
| reach one record                        | `hasOne(Author, { through: "article" })`                     |
| read the records                        | `tag.articles()`, a Relation                                 |
| preload them                            | `Tag.includes("articles")`                                   |
| link a record                           | `tag.articles().push(article)`                               |
| create a record and link it             | `tag.articles().create({ … })`                               |
| unlink a record                         | `tag.articles().delete(article)`                             |

### Options

<TypeTable

    source: {
      type: "string",

        "The join Model’s association to follow. Defaults to this association’s name, singular or plural.",
    },
    scope: {
      type: "(q) => Relation",
      description: "Narrows or orders the records, as on any association.",
    },
    dependent: {
      type: '"destroy" | "deleteAll"',
      description: "What destroying the owner does to the join records. hasMany only.",
    },
  }}
/>

`scope` works as it does on any association. See [Scoping associations](./associations#scoping-associations).

## Reading records

Read a through association as you would any `hasMany`:

<include>/_excerpts/pages/through-associations/examples/read.mdx</include>

- `through` names one of the Model’s own associations, here `taggings`. The join Model’s association with the same name, singular or plural, leads on to the records: `articles` follows each tagging’s `article`.
- `tag.articles()` is a Relation, with every Relation method, read in one query. Its writes go to the taggings, as [below](#writing-through-it).
- Each record comes back once, however many join records point at it.
- The scopes of both associations it goes through apply, and so does its own `scope`.

## Preloading records

## Naming the source

If the join Model’s association has another name, pass it as `source`, as `writers` does to follow each article’s `author`:

<include>/_excerpts/pages/through-associations/examples/source.mdx</include>

`writers` goes through `articles`, which goes through `taggings`. An association can go through any number of others this way.

## Going through a belongsTo or a hasOne

A `hasOne` can go through a `belongsTo`, as a tagging’s `author` does through its `article`:

<include>/_excerpts/pages/through-associations/examples/has-one.mdx</include>

`through` can name a `belongsTo`, a `hasMany`, or a `hasOne`, and the association it follows can be any of the three. A `hasOne` goes through a `belongsTo` or a `hasOne`.

## Writing through it

Add records with `push`, `build`, and `create`, which each make the join record that links the owner to the record:

<include>/_excerpts/pages/through-associations/examples/write.mdx</include>

- `push(article)` saves a tagging for each article, in one write. A new article is saved first.
- `build(attributes)` builds the article and its tagging. Saving the tag saves both. Saving the article on its own saves no tagging.
- `create(attributes)` saves the article, then its tagging. An invalid article comes back unsaved, and nothing is written.

On a tag that isn’t saved yet, `push` and `build` wait for the tag’s save. `create` throws `RecordNotSaved` there, as it does through a `hasMany`.

Records built or created through it take the equality values of its `scope`, and their join records take those of the association it goes through.

## Removing records

Removing records through the association removes their join records. The records themselves stay:

| Method             | Does                                                                                                |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `delete(article)`  | Deletes the tag’s taggings to the article, without callbacks                                        |
| `destroy(article)` | Destroys those taggings, running their callbacks, in one write                                      |
| `clear()`          | Deletes every tagging to the tag’s articles, without callbacks. The tag then has no articles loaded |
| `deleteAll()`      | Deletes the same taggings, and resolves to how many it deleted                                      |
| `destroyAll()`     | Destroys the same taggings, running their callbacks, and resolves to the articles                   |

An article the tag doesn’t reach removes nothing. To remove an article itself, destroy it: `await article.destroy()`. In a batch, pass the association to the write: `b.deleteAll(tag.articles())` deletes the tag’s taggings.

:::warning[A narrowed deleteAll or destroyAll removes the records themselves]
`updateAll`, and a narrowed `deleteAll` or `destroyAll`, act on the records, as any Relation’s do. `tag.articles().where({ title: "Draft" }).deleteAll()` deletes those articles for every tag, not the tag’s taggings.
:::

### Removing join records with dependent

A `hasMany` through a join Model takes `dependent: "destroy"` or `dependent: "deleteAll"`, which acts on its join records:

- With `"destroy"`, destroying the tag destroys its taggings, running their callbacks, and `delete` destroys them too.
- With `"deleteAll"`, destroying the tag deletes them without callbacks.
- `clear` and `deleteAll` always delete them without callbacks.

## Setting a hasOne through another

A `hasOne` that goes through another `hasOne` has `set`, and no `build` or `create`:

- `set(record)` points the owner’s join record at the record, or creates the join record if there isn’t one, with the equality values of the join association’s `scope`.
- `set(null)` destroys the join record.

## Spotting read-only associations

Writes need a join Model: the association has to go through a `hasMany` (a `hasOne`, for a `hasOne`), and follow a `belongsTo` from there, with neither going through another. Any other write throws `ReadonlyAssociation`, naming the association that stops it. `writers` is read-only, since it goes through `articles`, which goes through `taggings`.

`insert`, `insertAll`, `upsert`, and `upsertAll` throw `ReadonlyAssociation` on any association through another, since they write no join records.

:::note
Every association it goes through must be on one database. One that reaches [another database](./several-databases#associating-models-on-different-databases) is an `InvalidModel` for now ([#435](https://github.com/forestfuture/active-record/issues/435)).
:::

<Callout type="info" title="Compared with Rails" icon="/ruby.svg">

`through` and `source` work as Rails’ `has_many :through` and `has_one :through` options do. Rails reads through a SQL join, so a record two join records point at comes back twice unless the association is `distinct`. Here it comes back once.

Writing through it works as in Rails: `destroy` destroys the join records rather than the records, `clear` deletes them whatever `dependent` is, and `insert_all` through it raises. Rails also writes through a `belongsTo` or `hasOne` hop by building or updating that hop’s record, which can change a record that other records share. Here that throws `ReadonlyAssociation`. Rails’ `disable_joins` has no counterpart yet.

</Callout>
